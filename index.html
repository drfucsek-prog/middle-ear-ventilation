Welcome

This website is dedicated to the physiological mechanisms of middle ear ventilation and their potential clinical implications for chronic otitis media with effusion in children.

Its purpose is to make theoretical research freely accessible and available for scientific discussion and future investigation.

Theoretical Background

The proposed theory considers middle ear ventilation as a dynamic interaction between two complementary physiological mechanisms:

  1. Continuous transmucosal diffusion of oxygen and nitrogen.
  2. Intermittent pressure-driven gas exchange through the Eustachian tube.

The hypothesis is that these mechanisms jointly maintain middle ear pressure and a characteristic gas composition through a dynamic equilibrium.

Mathematical Model

The mathematical model investigates changes in oxygen and nitrogen partial pressures, transmucosal gas transport, intermittent Eustachian tube opening and total middle ear pressure.

The model aims to explain the establishment of a dynamic equilibrium under physiological conditions.

Detailed equations, simulations, tables and figures will be presented here.

Pressure-Controlled Valvular Tympanostomy Tube

A theoretical bidirectional, normally closed, pressure-controlled tympanostomy tube is proposed to reproduce selected physiological functions of intermittent Eustachian tube ventilation.

The proposed microvalve opens in response to a pressure difference and closes after pressure equalization.

This concept remains investigational and requires engineering and clinical validation.
